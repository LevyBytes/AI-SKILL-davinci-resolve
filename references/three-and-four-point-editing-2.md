### Replace Edits

A replace edit is a distinctive three-point edit type that lines up the frame under the Source Viewer playhead with the frame under the Timeline playhead at the moment the edit runs. It's the quickest edit type to reach for whenever you need to align an action at a particular frame of video, or a sound at a particular frame of audio, with a specific frame's action or sound in the Timeline's video or audio. The quickest route to a replace edit is to leave the Source Viewer's In and Out points unset altogether, letting either the duration of an existing clip that crosses the Timeline drive the edit, or a pair of timeline In/Out points that flag the part of a clip needing an overwrite, or else an empty Timeline span you'd like to edit into. The Timeline isn't rippled by replace edits. Replace Edits to Replace Existing Clips in the Timeline Automatically, a replace edit substitutes a Source Viewer clip for an existing Timeline clip, as long as that clip crosses the playhead and rests on a track whose destination control is on. When a replace edit goes this way, DaVinci Resolve picks up the Timeline clip's duration on its own to fix how long the incoming media runs, and reads where the Viewer and Timeline playheads sit to line up the placement of that incoming media. Being free of any In or Out points whatsoever, this is a remarkably fast edit to perform. To replace a clip in the Timeline: 1 Bring the Timeline playhead onto the clip you'd like to replace, parking it on a frame you want paired with a frame of the clip you're about to replace edit into the Timeline. 2 In the Source Track Selector, click the relevant audio and video destination controls for the track that holds the clip you mean to replace. 3 Open a clip into the Source Viewer. 4 Bring the Source Viewer playhead onto the frame you'd like paired with the frame beneath the Timeline playhead.

### Replace Edits to Edit Clips Into Empty Tracks

A replace edit serves just as well for editing a clip into a vacant Timeline track such that the frame beneath the Source playhead lines up with the Timeline playhead, while the incoming clip's In and Out points land wherever they may. This is handy whenever you'd like to "spot" some particular action of an alternate take, or a sound-effect cue, onto one specific Timeline frame. To apply a replace edit when spotting a sound effect or action video clip into the Timeline: 1 Bring the Timeline playhead onto the clip holding the moment the fresh incoming audio or video clip should align with, parking it on the precise frame you want paired with a frame of the clip about to be edited into the Timeline. 2 In the Source Track Selector, click the relevant audio and video destination controls for the vacant track the incoming clip should be edited into. 3 Open a clip into the Source Viewer. 4 Bring the Source Viewer playhead onto the frame you'd like paired with the frame beneath the Timeline playhead. ### Fit to Fill

Fit to fill edits are the sole edit type that genuinely use all four edit points, and the only edit type that retimes clips at the same moment they're being edited. By setting In and Out points on the incoming source clip, plus another pair of In and Out points on the Timeline, you can stretch or squeeze the timing of the marked range of source media so it covers the whole marked range of the Timeline. Along the way, the clip's speed ratio shifts so it plays in either fast or slow motion. Fit to fill edits prove especially valuable when you have a source clip whose action runs slightly slow and you simply want to speed it up by squeezing it into a shorter Timeline duration. They're also tremendously handy when you have a gap in an edited run of clips to fill with a source clip that just isn't long enough, but where slightly slower motion won't be noticeable. The Timeline isn't rippled by fit to fill edits. To apply fit to fill when editing a clip into the Timeline: 1 To flag where in the Timeline the incoming clip lands, do one of the following: a) Both Timeline In and Out points can be set to flag, as a three-point edit, the duration you'd like the incoming source clip to fill. b) The Timeline In and Out points can be cleared (via Option-X), so that instead you draw on the duration of whatever clip or gap the playhead crosses on the track whose destination controls are assigned within the Source Track Selector. ### Place on Top

Automatically, place on top edits superimpose clips onto the first vacant track sitting above (for video clips) or beneath (for audio clips) whatever other Timeline clips either cross the playhead or land between the currently set Timeline In and Out points, regardless of which track the Source Track Selector's destination controls happen to point at right now. It's built to make it easy to superimpose titles and other clips you want composited over another clip, or to add further versions of clips such as VFX atop earlier versions you want kept. Where needed, place on top edits spin up new timeline tracks, and the Timeline goes un-rippled. To apply place on top when editing a clip into the Timeline: 1 To settle where in the Timeline the clip ends up "placed on top," do one of the following: Move the playhead so it crosses the clip the incoming source clip should be edited over. Lay In and Out points on the Timeline marking the span within which the incoming source rides on top. 2 Lay In and Out points on a source clip you'd like to edit. 3 Pick Edit > Place on Top; alternatively drag any clip onto the Timeline Viewer's Place on Top overlay, or hit F12.

Before and after applying place on top, the incoming text generator rides superimposed onto a track sitting above the clip at the playhead's location

Incoming video clips get edited to the topmost video track so they sit above any video already in the Timeline. Incoming audio clips get edited to the bottom-most audio track so they sit below any audio already there. New video and/or audio tracks will be created automatically where needed to hold the new incoming clip.

### Ripple Overwrite

Ripple Overwrite is a four-point edit that comes in handy when you can pinpoint a Timeline segment you want to overwrite, but the incoming clip carries a different duration and you want DaVinci Resolve to ripple the Timeline automatically to absorb the difference. You can use the Ripple Overwrite command in one of two ways:

- An entire Timeline clip can be overwritten with a second clip whose length differs.
- A Timeline section flagged by In and Out points can be overwritten with a second clip whose

length differs. In both cases, every clip lying to the right of the overwritten clip or timeline section gets rippled rightward or leftward to free up room or close the gap. As a consequence, a ripple overwrite edit will in all likelihood alter the overall duration of your edited clip run. Using Ripple Overwrite on an Entire Clip in the Timeline Handling ripple overwrite as an automatic four-point edit, you can wholly overwrite, with the incoming clip, whatever Timeline clip crosses the playhead on the tracks fixed by the destination controls inside the Source Track Selector. The Timeline must hold no In or Out points for this to function. Once a ripple overwrite runs this way, the original timeline clip vanishes and the incoming clip slots into its place, with every clip to the right of the clip being replaced either (a) rippled rightward should the incoming clip exceed the original timeline clip in length, or (b) rippled leftward should the incoming clip fall short of the original timeline clip. The whole thing occurs in one step. This is useful when you'd like to swiftly trade one Timeline clip for another of unequal duration and let the Timeline open space for it automatically, the lot in a single move. To apply ripple overwrite when replacing an entire Timeline clip with a different source clip: 1 Move the Timeline playhead so it crosses the clip you're replacing; precisely where the playhead settles is unimportant. 2 In the Source Track Selector, click the relevant audio and video destination plus Sync Lock controls for the track that holds the clip you mean to replace, and press Option-X to wipe out whatever In and Out points the Timeline may hold. 3 Open a clip into the Source Viewer, setting In and/or Out points as required to flag how much of the clip should be edited into the Timeline. 4 To fire the edit, pick Edit > Ripple Overwrite; alternatively drag the clip to the Timeline Viewer's Ripple Overwrite overlay, or hit Shift-F10.

Once a ripple overwrite runs this way, the Timeline section flagged with In and Out points vanishes and the incoming clip slots into its place, with every clip to the right of the clip being replaced either (a) rippled rightward should the incoming clip exceed the original timeline clip in length, or (b) rippled leftward should the incoming clip fall short of the original timeline clip. A solid instance where this earns its keep is when you cut a close-up of an actor performing some action into a medium shot of that same actor performing the identical action already sitting in the Timeline, where the action you're matching runs a different length in each shot. ### Append to End

With append to end, the edited clip lands every time at the tail of the current Timeline. As an edit type it's tremendously handy while you rapidly chain a series of clips together. To apply append to end when editing a clip into the Timeline: 1 Lay In and Out points on a source clip you'd like tacked onto the end of the current Timeline. Where required, reorder the Media Pool's sort so these clips fall into the sequence you want them joined to the Timeline. 2 In the Source Track Selector, click the audio and video destination controls for whatever tracks the incoming source clip should sit on. Build new tracks if you require them. 3 Pick Edit > Append to End of Timeline; alternatively drag the clip to the Timeline Viewer's Append at end overlay, or hit Shift-F12. Incoming video clips join in directly past the very tail of the Timeline's last clip.

### Insert Selected Clips to Timeline Using Timecode

Clips can be edited straight from the Media Pool into a timeline so that each clip's source timecode lines up with a matching record timecode value in the Timeline. This is handy for long form multi-camera events, weddings or concerts say, where each camera runs the same timecode so every edit stays flawlessly synced. The Source Overwrite edit on the Cut page is what this function corresponds to. IMPORTANT: This edit only works where the Timeline's timecode overlaps the clip(s)' timecode. That gets set within the Start Timecode field of the New Timeline settings.

To insert selected clips to timeline using timecode: 1 In the Media Pool, select one or more clips to edit into the Timeline. If In and Out points are set on the clip, the edit respects those bounds. If no In/Out points are set, each selected clip gets edited in full. 2 Set a destination control to settle which Timeline track gets edited to. 3 Right-click any one of the selected clips and, off the drop-down menu, pick "Insert Selected Clips to Timeline Using Timecode." 4 Every selected clip gets overwritten into the Timeline at its appropriate timecode location onto the destination track. IMPORTANT: If multiple selected clips share overlapping timecode, no edit happens.

### Insert Selected Clips to Timeline With Handles

Reachable from the Media Pool contextual menu, "Insert Selected Clips to Timeline With Handles" is a command that edits one or more selected clips onto the open timeline while trimming the default handle length off both the head and tail of each clip. Its purpose is to simplify chaining together a run of clips you'd like joined by transitions, doing so by automatically reworking the In and Out points of every clip edited into the Timeline so that handles come into being. To apply insert selected clips to timeline with handles when editing one or more clips into the Timeline: 1 In the Media Pool, pick one or more clips you'd like joined to the Timeline. Where required, reorder the Media Pool's sort so these clips fall into the sequence you want them joined to the Timeline. 2 In the Source Track Selector, click the audio and video destination controls for whatever tracks the incoming source clip should sit on, and park the playhead at the spot the incoming clips should begin. Build new tracks if you require them. 3 Right-click any one of the selected clips in the Media Pool and, off the contextual menu, pick "Insert selected clips to timeline with handles." Those selected clips join the Timeline beginning at the playhead's location. To alter how long the handles being removed are, open the Editing panel of the User Preferences and adjust the "Default handles length" setting. There are two cases in which handles won't be added:

- Should any of the chosen Media Pool clips already bear handles from In and Out

points you've placed, then further handles get skipped.

- Should the span of the frames slated for removal to form handles in this operation exceed

the span of one or more clips you've chosen in the Media Pool, then no handles get added whatsoever.
